# EOV — Research Scope Lock

Decisions taken by the client, 2026-09-01. These override the open options in
`01-research-design.md`. Two tensions are flagged below and must be resolved at the
synthesis gate (§8 of the design), not before.

---

## 1. Geographic scope — LOCKED

**Primary universe: UAE + GCC.** The ranked top 15 is drawn from agencies that
compete for work in the UAE with GCC reach. Global groups qualify only through
their regional operating arms (Publicis Groupe MEA, VML/WPP MENA, Omnicom MENA,
Dentsu MENA, Havas Middle East, Accenture Song ME), assessed on regional capability
and not on global parent scale.

**Secondary: global benchmark panel.** A separate, unranked panel of 6–8 world-class
integrated agencies is researched alongside — not to be ranked against EOV, but to
**calibrate the integration-depth scale**. Without it, a 5/5 integration score means
"best in Dubai" rather than "world class", and the credentials deck inherits a
regional ceiling. The panel sets the top of the scale; the GCC 15 are scored against it.

Rationale for keeping these separate: mixing a global benchmark into a regional
ranking produces a list EOV cannot act on. Keeping them separate produces two usable
things — a real competitive set, and a real standard.

## 2. Ranking basis — LOCKED

**One flat ranking of 15, ordered by integration depth.** Not by revenue, headcount
or awards.

This is the most category-true basis available: the market EOV is entering is defined
by the word *integrated*, so the ranking measures the thing the category claims to
sell. It also permits a genuine finding that scale-based rankings suppress — that a
mid-sized independent can be more truly integrated than a large network arm running
siloed units under one logo.

**Integration Depth Score (IDS), 0–100.** Composite of three sub-scores, scored
identically for all 15 and for the global panel:

| Sub-score | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---:|---|
| **Disciplinary breadth** | 40% | Coverage across 11 disciplines: strategy · brand & creative · content & production · media planning & buying · performance/digital · social & influencer · CRM & martech · data & analytics · PR & comms · experiential & events · web/product build. Scored on evidence of delivery, not on a services page. |
| **Structural integration** | 35% | Single P&L vs federated units · one delivery team vs cross-selling silos · shared strategy layer · single point of client accountability. The most commonly falsified claim in the category, so scored hardest on evidence. |
| **Outcome accountability** | 25% | Contracted to business outcomes vs deliverables · retainer/AOR vs project · measurement ownership · published effectiveness proof (Effie-type, not craft awards). |

Supporting attributes — scale, roster, awards, public-sector credibility, growth
vectors, momentum — are **captured in full on every profile but do not drive rank
order.** They become the secondary lenses used in analysis and in the deck. Nothing
from the original scorecard is lost; it is demoted from ranking driver to profile field.

Two additional non-ranking fields are retained on every profile because they feed the
deck directly: **how EOV beats them** and **how they beat EOV**.

## 3. Target segments — LOCKED, WITH A FLAG

All four selected: government & public sector · large private enterprise ·
mid-market private · multinational regional hubs.

**Flag 1 — this is not yet a positioning decision, it is the absence of one.** Four
segments with different buying units, procurement routes, budget bands and decision
criteria cannot be served by a single proposition without diluting it to the point of
saying nothing. A ~10–50 person independent competing across all four simultaneously
is the position every undifferentiated agency occupies by default.

**How the research handles it — no work is lost either way.** All four are researched
in full through Workstream C, and each is run against the five segment screens
(reachable · distinct · definable · identifiable · profitable) plus an EOV-specific
sixth: **provable** — does EOV have citable evidence of winning in that segment? The
output is a ranked, evidenced case for which one or two lead and which are pursued
opportunistically. The prioritisation decision returns to the client at the synthesis
gate with the evidence attached, rather than being made now by assumption.

## 4. Deck purpose — REVISED AND LOCKED (client clarification)

**Primary: a universal introduction to any prospective client.** The deck is EOV's
front door — the first substantive thing a prospect of any type receives. **Secondary:
it must also stand up as government tender / pre-qualification material.**

This supersedes the earlier tender-primary answer, and **resolves Flag 2** — but in the
direction of the harder problem, not the easier one. The two jobs are still genuinely
different: a tender response is scored against a rubric by an evaluation panel working
a checklist; an introduction to a private CMO or an MNC hub has to earn attention and
build conviction. What has changed is that the narrative job is now primary and the
compliance job is a constraint on it, rather than the reverse.

**No deck structure is specified here.** How the deck is built is a Stage 2+ question
and is explicitly out of scope. The only thing this decision does is set what the
research must cover.

### Research consequence

Government tender criteria research **retains its high priority but loses its monopoly
on the deck's structure.** It remains uniquely valuable for a reason that has nothing
to do with tenders specifically: UAE and GCC government tenders **publish weighted
evaluation criteria**, which is the only place in this market where buying criteria are
stated literally with weights attached rather than inferred. It is the best available
evidence of what buyers actually score — a calibration instrument for the whole deck.

Alongside it, Workstream C must now cover **credentials-stage buying behaviour across
all four segments** with equal rigour: what a private-enterprise CMO, a mid-market
owner and an MNC regional lead each want from a first introduction, how long they give
it, what makes them take a second meeting, and what makes them discard an agency at
first contact. That research was previously scoped as secondary. It is now core.

**Note on the interaction with Flag 1:** a universal introduction deck across four
segments raises the cost of not prioritising. The spine must be true and compelling for
all four, which is achievable — but only if the positioning is built on something
genuinely segment-independent (how EOV works, what it has proved, what it is
accountable for) rather than on sector claims that ring hollow to three audiences out
of four. Flag 1 still resolves at the synthesis gate.

## 6. Unchanged

The evidence standard in §9 of the design applies without exception: no invented
figures, source and date on every material claim, confidence rating on every finding,
agency self-claims labelled as claims, contradictions surfaced.

Still blocked on client input: verified EOV baseline, client roster and citable case
evidence, commercial model, and `eov.ae` site content (egress-blocked from this
environment).
