# EOV — How Agencies Actually Communicate (large + mid-size)

Published: **https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0d457c43-6ae3-442d-9c6d-d19c7376ad38**

Supersedes the five "amazing deck" suggestions in conversation, all of which assumed
transparency is free. It is not — NDAs shape the entire category, and the generic tone
of large-agency decks is a rational response to a legal constraint, not laziness.

---

## 1. The mechanism

Reported blockers on agency proof are consistent: **strict NDAs, long project cycles,
multi-layered client approvals, competitive sensitivities, cautious legal departments.**
Standing advice: only show logos with written permission — a single cease-and-desist is
not worth the risk.

Large agencies therefore **cannot compete on specific, named, quantified proof.** Not
for lack of it — they are not permitted to use it.

> **When proof is unusable, differentiation moves to the operating model. That is why
> every holding company has a named one, and why almost no independent does.**

---

## 2. The named models

| Group | Named model | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Publicis** | **Power of One** | One client-facing operating approach across media, creative, production, data, technology. Positions as *"the system architect — the one designing how creativity, data and technology actually fit together."* |
| **Omnicom** | **Open Architecture + Omni** | Configurable team model plus a data platform explicitly described as **an operating system**. Post-IPG: *"an integrated operating company."* |
| **IPG** | **Open Architecture** | Teams assembled around the client, not the org chart |
| **Havas** | **Converged** | Data, content and media as one operating layer |
| **WPP** | *"A single, integrated company"* | Transitioning **from holding company to one company** — positioning as *the agency*, not the owner of agencies |

**They have converged on the same answer:** clients want simplification, capabilities
sold as one team at group level, distinctive brands retained underneath.

**Consequence: "integrated" is not a claim, it is the category premise.** Competition is
about whose version of integration convinces — and every serious player named theirs.

## 3. Mid-size independent positioning — uniform and commoditised

Near-verbatim across trade coverage:

- *"Holding-company-level talent with boutique-level attention."*
- *"In large networks, senior talent pitches and then hands the account to junior staff."*
- *"We don't have 15 rounds of internal alignment, and your account won't be handled by an algorithm."*
- *"The person who recommended the campaign is the person who loses sleep if it underperforms."*

Always the same three claims: **speed, senior attention, no bureaucracy.** Sometimes a
fourth: deep vertical knowledge.

**Why it fails:** every independent makes it, so it carries no discriminating
information. And structurally, **independents argue from what they are not** — a
negative position that depends on the listener already being unhappy with a network.
Against a prospect who isn't, it says nothing.

**Independents almost never have a named model.** They describe a *temperament* where
networks describe a *machine*.

## 4. The tonal split

| | Networks — system language | Independents — human language |
|---|---|---|
| **Nouns** | architecture, platform, operating system, convergence, capability, scale, transformation | partner, attention, relationship, craft, care, agility, founder |
| **Sentence subject** | the model | a person |
| **Register** | corporate, abstract, unhurried, deliberately impersonal | warm, direct, sometimes combative toward networks |
| **Proof** | scale, roster breadth, awards, group revenue | named individuals, tenure, retention, testimonials |
| **Risk managed** | looking small or improvised | looking junior or under-resourced |

**Shared convention worth copying:** agencies that win consistently **open with a point
of view or philosophy, not founding-year facts.** 10–15 slides.
Challenge–solution–outcome. Weak decks present logos and vague results.

## 5. How they handle work they cannot name

- **Sector-and-scale substitution** — "a GCC government entity", "a 1906 Swiss
  manufacturer". Specificity lives in the description, not the label.
- **Named challenge, unnamed client** — the problem and approach are usually not
  confidential even when the client is. Detail migrates from *who* to *what*.
- **Indexed rather than absolute numbers** — percentages, multiples and ratios are often
  permitted where revenue figures are not.
- **Gated deeper proof** — password-protected portfolio, named recipients, redacted names.
- **Logo discipline** — written permission only.

### The convention EOV can exploit
Because everyone is constrained identically, **the differentiator is not more proof —
it is proof of a different kind.** Publicly verifiable evidence sidesteps NDAs entirely:
search visibility, a documented Expo pavilion, a live storefront, externally reported
launch coverage. None requires client permission because none is the client's private
information. Structural advantage over both tiers, available now.

## 6. Conclusions for EOV

### 1. EOV needs a named model and does not have one
The clearest gap the research exposes. Networks compete on named models; independents
compete on temperament and lose. EOV has the *components* — governing standard,
measurement gate, delivery architecture — but no name holding them together and no
single idea a prospect repeats after the meeting.

Three-stage naming set aside per client steer. **The naming question is open and should
be answered from the assets below, not invented.**

### 2. The strongest unclaimed asset is client-owned infrastructure
EOV's deck already states platforms, ad accounts, analytics and CRM are **created in the
client's name and remain under the client's control.** Against networks — where account
ownership and data custody are a live industry grievance — that is structural,
contractual and verifiable.

It is also **generically statable**: no client name, no metric, no permission required.
And expensive to copy, because it removes a lock-in most agencies rely on.

### 3. Use system language at independent scale
The independent register (attention, care, partnership) is the commodity argument and
where EOV disappears. The network register (architecture, governance, operating
standard) is where EOV's actual assets live, and almost no independent occupies it
credibly.

> **Sound like a system, delivered by people you can name.** Networks have the system
> language but cannot promise the people. Independents promise the people but have no
> system to describe. Neither tier holds that position.

### Keep / break
**Keep:** open with a point of view · 10–15 slides · challenge–solution–outcome ·
sector-and-scale substitution · strict logo discipline · system-register vocabulary.

**Break:** the independent's negative positioning. Do not argue from what EOV is not.
