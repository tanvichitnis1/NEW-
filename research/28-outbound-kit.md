# 28 · Outbound Kit — Motion B

**Artifact:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9eb0ce54-9461-46b5-96aa-76a4f5309787

Builds items 1–3 of the "mine, unblocked" list in `23`/`27`. Serves Motion B
from `24`: cold outbound to private B2B, B2C and D2C companies.

**Premise:** every agency emailing that marketing director this week is also
integrated, experienced and results-driven. The only thing that earns a reply
is a specific, checkable observation about their own business — and `15`
proved EOV can produce one from a search pull alone.

---

## 1 · The audit — five checks, twenty minutes, one finding

Not a report. Looking for **the single most valuable sentence.** Stop when you
have one worth sending.

| # | Check | Run | Flag if | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Traffic concentration** | Organic Research → Pages by traffic share (`resource_organic_unique`) | One page >40% of organic traffic | The catalogue is invisible |
| 2 | **Brand cannibalisation** | Search exact brand name, count their own competing URLs | More than 3 of their URLs rank for their own name | Competing with themselves on the one term they should own |
| 3 | **Duplicate host/protocol** | Does `http://`, `http://www` or a staging host still rank? | Any measurable share on a non-canonical version | Pure leakage from a missing redirect. Free to fix — most persuasive of the five |
| 4 | **Paid against organic** | Paid keywords vs terms already top-3 organic | Buying terms they already own | Named, quantifiable waste. Turns marketing into budget in one line |
| 5 | **Category absence** | Keyword Gap vs two named competitors | No ranking on a category term a competitor owns | Weakest — names a competitor, can put people on the defensive. Use only if 1–4 come back clean |

**Example sentences** (real results of the `15` audit, as illustrations of
form — not claims about any prospect):

> "One product page is carrying 59% of your search traffic. The rest of the
> range is effectively invisible."
> "Nine of your own pages are competing for your brand name."
> "An unredirected http://www version is absorbing about 11% of your visits.
> That is a one-line fix."

**If all five come back clean, do not invent a sixth.** Send the sector
one-pager with a short note and move on. A manufactured finding is worse than
no finding.

## 2 · The emails

Under 120 words. **No attachment on the first send** — an attachment from an
unknown sender is a reason not to open it. Ask is twenty minutes, not a
capabilities meeting.

### Email 1 · with a finding (the one that works)
Subject: the finding in six words.
Structure: the finding with its number → **how they verify it themselves** →
one line on EOV → one proof point → ask for twenty minutes.

**The verification line is the part that works.** Telling them how to check it
themselves separates this from every other agency email: it says the finding
is real and is not being held back as bait.

### Email 2 · no finding (fallback, lower reply rate)
Who EOV is → one matched case → one page attached → *"if there is a live brief
I am happy to look at it; if not, no follow-up from me."*

### Email 3 · follow-up
Once, six working days later, **as a reply on the original thread**. Add one
thing — a second finding, or the fix for the first. Close with an explicit
permission to decline; it raises reply rates because it makes replying cheap.

Full copy in the artifact.

## 4 · Running it

- **Volume:** 10–15 a week, audited. Not 100 unaudited — the finding is the
  entire mechanism and does not survive being skipped.
- **Who:** marketing director or owner. Not `info@`.
- **How:** from a person, on the normal domain. No tracking pixels, no
  mail-merge footer, no unsubscribe block — all three mark it as bulk.
- **Log every audit**, including the ones with no finding. After 30 prospects
  you know which of the five checks fires most often in this market, and that
  tells you where the demand actually is.
- **The deck goes out when a meeting is agreed**, not before (`25`).
