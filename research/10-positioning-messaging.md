## 1. Positioning statement — REVISED

**Client correction (Sept 2026):** the audience is not government-led. It is **SMEs,
large enterprises, B2B, B2C and government**. This resolves the Stage 2 lead-position
question in favour of the **discipline** position, because a government-fluency lead
would be irrelevant or alienating to four of the five audiences.

| | |
|---|---|
| **For** | organisations across the UAE and GCC that have to justify what they spend on marketing — government entities, large enterprises, B2B firms and growing businesses alike |
| **Who** | need strategy, brand, platform, communications and demand run as one programme, with a traceable line from spend to outcome |
| **Our** | EOV is an Emirati-founded integrated marketing agency, working in the UAE since 2006 |
| **That provides** | the full capability set under one accountable relationship, sequenced so that decisions come before spend |
| **Unlike** | agencies that sell channels before defining what they are meant to move, and networks that fragment delivery across separate units |
| **Our USP** | **No media runs until tracking is verified. Every claim carries a source before it is published.** |

### The discriminating line

> **We don't run media until we can prove what it moved.**

**Why this works across all five audiences.** The unifying condition is not sector, it is
**accountability for spend** — true of an owner spending their own money, a
CFO-scrutinised enterprise, a B2B firm with a long attribution cycle, and a government
entity answerable publicly. The Tracking Gate answers all of them with one rule.

**Why it is defensible.** No agency in the competitive set — Publicis included — states a
hard rule refusing media spend until measurement is verified. It is falsifiable,
operational, and it costs EOV revenue to honour, which is what makes it credible where
"results-driven" is not.

**Why not the institutional lead.** `12` established that EOV's citable proof is
overwhelmingly commercial. This correction adds a second, independent reason: even if the
government roster were nameable, leading on it would misaddress most of the audience.
**Government becomes one proof module among several, not the position.**

## 4. Three message pillars

Emirati ownership and 20 years are deliberately **not** pillars. They are the reason to
believe running beneath all three — a credential, not a proposition.

### Pillar 1 — "We know how it gets approved"
- **Removes:** the risk of a programme stalling in approvals, or a bid scoring badly before the work is read
- **Proof required:** named entities and tenure; ICV certification; framework/panel status; programmes cleared with the approval route described
- **Failure mode:** saying "we understand government" — generic, unfalsifiable, universal. Specificity *is* the value.

### Pillar 2 — "Advice you don't have to hand to someone else"
- **Removes:** fragmentation; intent lost between the agency that recommends and the supplier that executes
- **Proof required:** multi-discipline programmes delivered end to end, named, with strategy and build made explicit
- **Failure mode: leading with this.** Boopin owns the sharper version at larger scale. Only defensible *after* Pillar 1 — institutional delivery, not delivery in general.

### Pillar 3 — "Decisions before spend"
- **Removes:** paying for channels before establishing what the programme is for or how it is judged
- **Proof required:** the model stage by stage — what is decided, produced, measured, and what triggers the next stage
- **Failure mode:** leaving it as three words on a slide. Unarticulated it is decoration; articulated it is the only named method EOV owns.

**Order is load-bearing.** Pillar 1 gets EOV into the consideration set — the binding
constraint in a ~1,600-agency market. Pillar 2 wins the work once in the room. Pillar 3
makes both credible. Reversing them puts EOV into a fight it loses.

## 6. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "You're smaller than the networks." | Correct — and the people in this room are the people on the account. The industry's most persistent client complaint is that the senior team presented is not the team delivered. Small is how that promise is kept. |
| "You're a government agency — do you understand commercial?" | Public-sector work has the hardest constraints in the market: multiple stakeholders, compliance, scrutiny, defined outcomes. Commercial briefs get the same discipline with fewer obstacles. |
| "We don't see award-winning creative." | Point to what was optimised for instead — approved, delivered, measured, renewed — and to retention as the harder metric. **Do not fake creative pedigree; award records are verifiable.** |
| "Why not a specialist per discipline?" | Because someone then has to integrate them, and that someone is you. Fragmentation is a cost paid in your time. |
| "How are you different from Boopin?" | Hardest question in the set. Answer on institutional depth and ownership, never on integration — they say integration better. **If Boopin proves Emirati-owned, this answer needs rebuilding.** |
| "You started as a production and technology company." | Yes — which is why the strategy does not stop at a recommendation. Reframe heritage as delivery capability; never defend it as the offer. |

## 7. Language rules

**Say:** named frameworks, entities and routes · "approved", "cleared", "renewed",
"retained" · specific years and tenure · "built and run" rather than "delivered" ·
named individuals with named responsibilities · numbers defendable under questioning.

**Never say:** "Strategy. Branding. Digital. Social. PR. Performance. Events." ·
"full-service" / "360°" / "one-stop shop" · "we understand government" ·
"results-driven" / "data-driven" / "innovative" · "your trusted partner" ·
any figure that cannot survive one follow-up question.

The second list carries no discriminating information in a ~1,600-agency market, and a
wrong or undefendable figure loses the meeting outright. The downside is asymmetric.

## 9. The new risk created by a five-segment audience

**The audience is wide and the proof is narrow.** SMEs, large enterprises, B2B, B2C and
government buy very differently, and EOV's evidenced case base is currently luxury
retail (Forrey & Galland) and industrial-to-consumer (Ferronato). There is no named SME
case, no named B2B case, and no nameable government case.

That gap is where a prospect will push: *"who like us have you done this for?"*

**The discipline position survives this** — a rule about how work is run is
segment-independent in a way a sector claim is not. But the **proof modules must flex**,
and building one credible case per priority segment is now the highest-value gap in the
evidence base after the campaign capture grid.

Note also the market finding this runs against: specialist capabilities are growing at
~16.5% CAGR against ~4.68% for the market. A five-segment audience is the opposite of
specialisation. It is defensible **only** if the proposition is a genuinely distinctive
*how* — which the Tracking Gate is, and which "strategy, branding, digital, social, PR,
performance, events" is not.
