**Status:** options for decision. Nothing here is invented — every candidate is

## 1 · Why a name at all

`19` established the convention: networks compete on **named operating models**
— Power of One (Publicis, four solution hubs beneath it), Open Architecture
(Omnicom, containing Omni), Converged (Dentsu), WPP's "single integrated
company". The name is not decoration. It is the mechanism by which a
procurement lead who was not in the room can repeat what the agency does.

EOV has the *components* of a model — a tracking gate, a six-point governing
standard, a delivery architecture, client-owned infrastructure — and no name
holding them together. The user's instruction was explicit: **forget the
Foundation → Launch → Always-On naming**. This document replaces it.

---

## 2 · The five tests

A name failing any of the first three is not worth adopting.

| # | Test | What it means |
|---|------|---------------|
| 1 | **True today** | Falsifiable, and already how EOV works. A model describing an ambition is a liability the first time a client tests it. |
| 2 | **Repeatable** | The prospect can say it accurately to a colleague who was not in the room. This is the entire mechanism. |
| 3 | **System-register** | Describes a machine, not a temperament. Warmth is the independent's commodity argument (`19`); EOV's assets are structural. |
| 4 | **Uncontested** | Not Power of One, Open Architecture, Converged, or a near-miss of any of them. |
| 5 | **Hard to copy** | Costs something to honour. A rule with no price attached is a slogan. |

## 3 · The asset base

Everything below is drawn from these, all present in the existing deck:

- Strategy before spend
- Tracking before launch (**the tracking gate**)
- Claims before reach
- The six-point governing standard — measurement, claims, brand governance,
  privacy and consent, CRM hygiene, executive reporting
- **Client-owned infrastructure** — platforms, ad accounts, analytics and CRM
  created in the client's name and remaining under client control
- One brief across every discipline; single senior accountability
- *"Breadth without discipline is still fragmentation."*

## 4 · The six options

### A · Built In Your Name — *ownership territory*
> Everything the engagement builds is created in the client's name and stays there.

- **Rests on:** client-owned infrastructure. Stated in the current deck,
  almost entirely unclaimed in the competitive set.
- **Why it works:** attacks the industry's live grievance — account ownership
  and data custody sitting with the agency. Contractual, verifiable, and
  expensive to copy, because it removes a lock-in most agencies quietly rely
  on. Needs no client name or metric to state, sidestepping the
  confidentiality constraint identified in `19`.
- **Risk:** reads as a policy rather than a model. Needs a container above it.

### B · Order of Operations — *sequence territory*
> The order is the method. Nothing is bought before it is decided; nothing runs before it is measured.

- **Rests on:** strategy before spend · tracking before launch · claims before
  reach — three commitments already written as an ordering rule.
- **Why it works:** borrowed from mathematics and engineering — exactly the
  register networks occupy and independents avoid. Uncontested. Makes three
  existing slogans legible as one idea.
- **Risk:** cool to the point of austere. Arguably a feature given where the
  competitive set sits.

### C · Verified Before Live — *verification territory*
> Measurement is built and confirmed before the first impression is bought.

- **Rests on:** the tracking gate plus the measurement architecture in the
  governing standard.
- **Why it works:** the single most differentiated commitment in the deck. No
  agency in the set (`08`) states a hard verification condition on spend.
  Framed as a standard rather than a refusal, it reads as rigour.
- **Risk:** narrow. Names a gate, not a model — cannot carry brand, creative,
  events or communications beneath it.

### D · One Standard — *governance territory*
> One governing standard applied across every discipline, in-house and specialist alike.

- **Rests on:** the six-point governing standard, described in the deck as
  *one governing standard*.
- **Why it works:** directly answers *"breadth without discipline is still
  fragmentation."* EOV's own language; the most extensible container here.
- **Risk:** **proximity to Power of One.** Close enough that a media-literate
  buyer may hear it as derivative — worse than having no name.

### E · On The Record — *evidence territory*
> Every material claim carries a source before it is published.

- **Rests on:** claims before reach; the claims-and-compliance review.
- **Why it works:** unusually relevant to regulated, institutional and B2B
  buyers, where an unsourced claim is a liability, not a style question.
- **Risk:** reads as a PR discipline, not an operating model. Describes one of
  the six standards, not the system.

### F · One Brief — *integration territory*
> Every discipline briefed from the same strategy, by the same team.

- **Rests on:** "one EOV brief"; single senior accountability.
- **Risk:** **contested ground — do not lead here.** `08` found Boopin already
  states the one-accountable-relationship claim verbatim, at larger scale,
  with overlapping government logos. It is also the commodity argument every
  independent makes (`19`). Fails the uncontested test outright.

## 6 · Recommendation — one container, three commitments

No single name carries the whole model, and forcing one to would either narrow
it or make it vague. The networks solved this the same way (`19`): **Power of
One contains four solution hubs; Open Architecture contains Omni.** Container
plus named components.

**Container: Order of Operations.** Breadth is only credible if it is
controlled — so the order in which things happen is the method.
*Attacks: agencies that sell channels before defining what they are meant to move.*

| | Commitment | What it says | What it attacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** | **Sequence** | Nothing is bought before it is decided. Market evidence, objective, positioning and measure of success are agreed and written down first. | Channel-led selling. |
| **02** | **Verification** | Nothing runs before it can be measured. The source-to-action route is confirmed before the first impression is bought. | Unmeasurable spend. Uncontested across the entire set. |
| **03** | **Title** | Everything built is built in your name. Platforms, ad accounts, analytics and CRM are created under client control and stay there. | Network lock-in and data custody. Hardest of the three to copy. |

**Why this shape.** It is repeatable at two levels. A prospect who remembers
nothing else remembers *"they don't run media until it's measurable, and they
build everything in your name."* Two sentences, both verifiable, neither
requiring a client reference to state — which matters given the
confidentiality constraint that ruled out the earlier transparency ideas.

It also lets the five practices sit underneath as **the work** rather than the
pitch — the original structural problem, solved without a stage-naming
convention.

## 7 · Required before adoption

1. **Trademark and prior-use check.** "Order of Operations" and "On The Record"
   are common phrases and may be in use by agencies or martech firms. Search
   before committing; check UAE registration if the name will appear in tender
   documents. **Not carried out.**
2. **Arabic test.** Each must survive translation and be sayable to an
   Arabic-first stakeholder. "Order of Operations" and "Built In Your Name"
   translate as concepts; "On The Record" is idiomatic English and probably
   does not. Relevant given the Arabic gap in `06` (63% prefer Arabic,
   <20% of sites optimised). **Not carried out.**
3. **The honesty test — client input required.** Adopt *Verification* only if
   EOV will genuinely decline to run media without tracking. A named
   commitment that bends the first time a client pushes is worse than no name.
   Clients test these in month three. This is on the open-questions list
   already: *does EOV genuinely hold the verification line?*
