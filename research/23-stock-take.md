---

## 1 · The correction

The work had been framed as a **differentiation problem** — find a claim
nobody else makes, make it falsifiable, prove it with artefacts, build the
deck on it. That produced `20`–`22` and dead-ended, because the claim chosen
was sharp enough to require proof EOV does not hold.

**Wrong frame, and the research had already said so:**

- `19` found large and mid-size agencies deliberately keep communications
  generic, because confidentiality prevents anything else.
- `04` found credentials-stage buyers want three things: *can you do this,
  have you done it before, will you be straightforward to work with.* None
  asked for a proprietary methodology.

A named operating model is a **network's** solution to a **network's**
problem — explaining why twenty subsidiaries behave as one company. EOV is one
company and does not have that problem.

**EOV already holds ordinary differentiators that are free, true, and need no
evidence trail:** Emirati-founded, working since 2006, both Abu Dhabi and
Dubai, genuinely broad in-house, clients across retail, luxury goods,
industrial B2B and government. Stated plainly, that is a strong position.

The tracking discipline stays — as *how EOV works*, in the process section,
like any competent agency. Not as the thing the deck stands on.

## 3 · Two real problems in `10`

### 3.1 The core narrative still opens on government
`10` §1 was corrected in September for the five-segment audience. **§2, the
core narrative, was never updated** — it still opens *"Government
communications in the UAE is no longer an informal craft…"* and runs four
paragraphs on regulatory fluency.

That narrative is what deck copy gets written from. Left alone the deck opens
on government to an audience four-fifths not government — the exact error the
correction was made to fix. **Highest-priority rewrite, needs nothing from
EOV.**

### 3.2 The USP is staked on the one thing that cannot be shown
`10` sets the discriminating line as *"we don't run media until we can prove
what it moved."* Under the correction this is simply **over-claimed** — EOV
cannot produce the records (`22`), and staking a deck on an unshowable claim
is risk, not reward.

**Fix is demotion, not deletion.** Tracking discipline moves into the process
section as a normal statement of how EOV works. No gate, no rule, no proof
burden. **This removes the audit from the critical path entirely.**

### 3.3 Still urgent, unrelated
eov.ae says *"digital media and technology agency"*; the deck says integrated
marketing agency (`17`). A prospect who reads the deck then visits the site
sees two companies and believes the site.

## 5 · What the deck becomes

Ordinary, in the way good credentials decks are ordinary — the pattern `19`
already documented.

1. **Who we are** — Emirati-founded, since 2006, both emirates, scale and shape
2. **What we do** — disciplines stated plainly, in-house vs partnered said honestly
3. **How we work** — short, unbranded process: strategy before spend, measurement set up before launch, client owns what gets built. Three sentences, no model name, no proof burden
4. **Who we do it for** — sectors and clients; government as one of several, not the position
5. **The work** — Ferronato, Forrey & Galland, KGS, the EOV platform. Longest section; carries the deck
6. **The people** — who actually runs the account
7. **How to start** — one slide, one contact

`13`'s fifteen-slide structure already fits. Act 2 shrinks, the work section
grows. No re-architecture.
