## 1 · Lock-in, in plain terms

A client's advertising accounts, analytics, pixels and CRM have to be created
under *someone's* business entity — the agency's or the client's.

Under the **agency's**: the client cannot leave cheaply. Firing the agency
means losing campaign history, conversion data and accumulated audiences, and
starting from zero. Many clients stay another year rather than rebuild. That
is lock-in — retention purchased by **switching cost**, not by quality.

Under the **client's**: they can leave tomorrow and take everything.

**The decision for Mohamed:** *are you willing to make it easy for clients to
leave EOV?* That is what rule 03 commits to.

- **Gain:** a very strong argument to new clients, and one most competitors
  will not make because they rely on the friction.
- **Cost:** a retention crutch, permanently. Revenue that currently stays
  partly out of inconvenience must stay on merit instead.

It is an ownership decision because it changes how the business holds revenue.

## 2 · Container, in plain terms

A container is an umbrella name for the method — Publicis's "Power of One". A
label a client can file EOV under and repeat later.

| | With a name | Without (option zero) |
|---|---|---|
| **What is said** | "Our method is The Same Standard. It has three rules." | "We don't buy before it's defined. We don't launch before it's measured. We don't build anything in our own name." |
| **Gains** | Survives retelling when a procurement officer summarises four agencies to a committee | No legal or imitation risk; three plain sentences beat any umbrella for recall |
| **Risks** | Collision, or sounding like an imitation of a network model | Nothing for the committee to file EOV under |

**"Free to reverse"** means: if the name never goes on a logo, into a
trademark filing, or into distinct typography — only used consistently in
conversation and on a page — adding or dropping it later costs nothing.
Rules 02 and 03 are the opposite: they change how the business runs and cannot
be quietly withdrawn once clients have been told.

## 4 · The evidence may not be lost

No written record was kept. That is not the same as no evidence existing.

**The platforms keep their own timestamps.** GA4 records property creation and
conversion event configuration dates. Google Ads and Meta record when a
campaign first served. Tag managers keep dated version history of every
container publish.

So for any past engagement: **was tracking configured before the first
impression served?** If the tag manager publish and conversion setup dates
precede campaign start, the gate was honoured and the platforms prove it —
whether or not anyone wrote it down. **Stronger** than a self-written record,
because EOV did not author it.

Worth checking before concluding the claim is dead. An afternoon per account,
and it may convert the "no" into a demonstrable record across the back book.

*Assessment rests on how these platforms are known to record configuration and
version history; confirm against EOV's actual accounts before relying on it.*

## 7 · The Pre-Launch Verification Record

One page, buildable this month, needed under either branch. A **blank** one
can be shown in a meeting — it proves the machine exists as a design,
discloses nothing about any client, and needs nobody's permission. This
resolves the `19` confidentiality constraint.

Fields:

- Client & engagement
- Campaign / flight
- Objective & success measure (ref. to definition stage)
- Conversion events configured — list, configured on (date)
- Source-to-action route tested — test event, result, date
- Consent & privacy check — pass/fail, date
- Reporting destination live — dashboard, confirmed on (date)
- Asset ownership confirmed — all data-capturing assets under client entity, Y/N
- **Exceptions carried** — none / launch proceeding unverified, see disclosure
- Verified by · date
- Client acknowledgement

The **exceptions** line is what makes it survive contact with reality. A fixed
launch date that cannot move (`16` — government event, retail moment,
lunar-calendar occasion) is handled by recording the exception and labelling
that flight's reporting *unverified attribution*, per the bend test in `21` §8
— not by quietly abandoning the rule.

*Proposed template. Not an existing EOV document.*
