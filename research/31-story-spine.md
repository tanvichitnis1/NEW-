# 31 · The Story Spine — narrative, not a parts list

**Artifact:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/c974c540-94bd-4be3-8541-a328712e9674

**Client direction:** *"Storytelling, narrative and how to piece things
together is art."* And, separately: the research in `29` was not deep enough.

Both correct. `29` inventoried twelve components and audited against them —
which is exactly the mechanical thinking that produces a competent,
forgettable document. This replaces the frame.

**Also confirmed by client:** KGS and Ferronato are **two brands in the same
group**. The `18`/`25` "one relationship" framing is correct and the
discrepancy flagged in `30` §5 is resolved.

---

## 1 · Why the standard frameworks do not transfer

**Duarte's Sparkline** — oscillating "what is" / "what could be" — builds
tension through *delivery*: pacing, pause, a speaker present. A credentials
deck is read alone, silently, on a phone, at speed (`25`). The oscillation
reads as repetition when nobody is performing it.

**Minto's SCQA** resolves to a single recommendation. A credentials document
recommends nothing yet. Forced into that shape it produces what every agency
deck produces: a manufactured problem the agency happens to solve.

The reader is neither an audience nor a decision-maker mid-decision. **They are
a person alone, running an interrogation.**

---

## 2 · The five questions, in the order they arrive

| Beat | Question | What it sets up |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Orientation** | Who is this? | Answer in one line and abandon it. Most decks stay here — history, values, origin — while the reader has moved on |
| **2 · Recognition** | Do they understand my world? | Marketing bought in pieces, nobody answerable. Recognisable because they are living it → *so what do you do differently?* |
| **3 · The turn** | What is your actual claim? | Breadth alone is noise. The claim must be narrower and harder → *prove it* |
| **4 · Evidence** | Can they actually do it? | The long stretch. Ordered by rising stakes → *but are they safe?* |
| **5 · Risk** | Is hiring them survivable? | How it runs, who is accountable, what the client owns → *what happens next?* |

The art is answering each in a way that **raises** the next.

---

## 3 · The through-line

What EOV's four cases actually share:

- A national event staged in Vienna on a fixed date, with a minister present
- Occasion retail against a lunar calendar that moves ~11 days a year and
  arrives whether you are ready or not (`16`)
- A category-first brand launched into an Expo window (`30`)
- A shielding material specified in decibels, where tolerance is engineering,
  not opinion

> **None of it could be redone.**

**Claim:** *Twenty years of work that had to be right the first time.*

Why it fits: `29` established that a credentials document exists to reduce the
perceived risk of hiring. An agency whose entire portfolio is work that got one
attempt answers that with **evidence rather than assurance**.

It also earns the breadth claim honestly — not a service list, but a
ministerial event abroad, a technical B2B category, a luxury launch and a
retail calendar, none resembling each other, handled by one team.

## 4 · Escalation — the case order reverses

`29` ranked cases by how good the story was. Wrong criterion. **Order by rising
stakes**, because the last one read is the one remembered.

| Order | Case | Objection it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Forrey & Galland** — recurring commercial job | *Can they run a business's marketing?* Most familiar shape; the reader maps it onto their own operation without effort. Sets the competence baseline |
| **2** | **KGS + Ferronato** — range inside one exhibit | *But can they do my kind of thing?* One owner, one material, two opposite markets. The only case that **demonstrates** breadth rather than claiming it — so it must sit adjacent as one arc, never split by another client |
| **3** | **OPEC, UAE Night Vienna** — the stakes | *Can I trust them with something that matters?* A nation represented abroad, minister present, date immovable. Highest stakes, so it goes last and becomes the note the reader leaves on |

**This reverses `30`'s conclusion** that Ferronato leads. Ferronato stays in
the deck and keeps its results — it moves inside the group arc at position two.

---

## 5 · Where the art actually lives

Three things separate narrative from a well-organised list. None are visible as
sections, which is why component checklists miss them.

1. **Every section ends by opening the next.** The clients slide should leave
   the reader asking *what did they actually do for them* — which the cases
   answer. A section that closes cleanly has stopped the document.
2. **The claim is made once, then only demonstrated.** Say "no second attempt"
   at the turn and never repeat it. Three cases prove it silently. A claim
   restated is a claim being defended.
3. **The last line of the last case is the argument** — not the contact slide.
   By the time the reader reaches "how to start", the decision is made.
   Everything after Vienna is administration.

---

## 6 · Consequences for the deck

- **Case order:** F&G → KGS+Ferronato arc → OPEC. Reverses `25` and `30`.
- **OPEC moves from optional to structural.** It is the closing argument, so
  consent for it is now a higher priority than it was in `27` §6.
- **`29` §3.2** (demote Ferronato for lacking a result) — already withdrawn by
  `30`; Ferronato's position is now set by escalation, not by its metrics.
- The KGS + Ferronato arc must stay **adjacent**, as restructured in `30`.

## 7 · Caveats

Sparkline is Nancy Duarte's framework; SCQA is Barbara Minto's. Both are
described from published summaries — the judgement that neither transfers to a
read-alone credentials document is mine. The through-line in §3 is an
interpretation of EOV's four cases, not a claim EOV has made, and should be put
to Mohamed before it is built on. Creative Review's article on credentials
decks was unreachable from this environment (egress blocked).
