Supersedes `11-deck-blueprint.md`.
Published: **https://claude.ai/code/artifact/6aad0428-e455-405f-9682-17c8467a7a73**

Client decision: logical sequencing, and case evidence limited to **the EOV website,
Ferronato, and Forrey & Galland**.

---

## 1. What the cut achieves

The existing deck carries ~15 client names across four slides, qualified three times:
*"across EOV and our specialist partners"* · *"figures as reported by the delivering
agency"* · *"programmes operated by the senior team"* — sitting a few pages from
*"One contract. One team. One point of accountability."*

**Cutting to three EOV-owned engagements removes every qualifier.** It also removes
almost every number (Ecostore, Honor, Yves Rocher), because those were the
partner-delivered ones.

**The proof standard therefore changes from *reported* to *inspectable*.** Two live
webshops, a live platform and a documented Expo 2020 pavilion are verifiable by the
reader in ten minutes. For an audience told "results-driven" by every agency it has met,
inspectable beats impressive.

It also brings the evidence in line with EOV's own governing standard — *claims before
reach: every material claim carries a source before it is published.* A deck quoting
another agency's figures was not applying EOV's own rule.

## 2. Sequencing logic — five acts

Each act exists because the reader cannot accept the next without it. The transitions
are the questions the reader is holding.

| Act | Content | Leaves the reader asking |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · The problem** | Marketing loses value between disciplines | "So what would fix that?" |
| **2 · The answer** | One accountable agency; three stages; five practices | "Every agency says that. Why believe you?" |
| **3 · The control** | Tracking gate; governing standard; delivery architecture | "Has it actually worked?" |
| **4 · The evidence** | Three inspectable cases + OPEC | "Who am I dealing with, and what now?" |
| **5 · Company and ask** | Heritage, leadership, next step | — |

### Four changes to the existing running order

1. **Problem before proposition.** Currently claims on p02, explains the problem on p03.
   Reversed, the proposition arrives as an answer rather than an assertion.
2. **Five practice slides collapse to one.** Consecutive practice pages read as a menu —
   the exact form the market cannot distinguish. Practices belong inside the stages.
3. **Governance moves up, p17 → Act 3.** The tracking gate is EOV's most differentiated
   asset and currently sits after fourteen slides of capability.
4. **Company facts move down, p06–08 → Act 5.** Credentials support a case; they cannot
   open one.

## 4. The three cases

Each proves a different practice; together they cover platform, brand/market entry, and
running a business end to end.

### The EOV platform — Practice 03
- **Earns its place:** the only case a reader can audit while reading. An agency claiming
  sites should behave like decision engines is immediately checkable against its own.
- **Must show:** IA and structured data as built, the measurement layer, what it is
  engineered to be found by — people and answer engines.
- **Risk:** it sets the bar for eov.ae. **If the site does not currently meet the
  Practice 03 standard, this slide is a liability.** Fix first or defer.

### Ferronato — Practices 01 + 02
- **Earns its place:** industrial manufacturer entering a consumer category is the
  hardest brand problem, and the Swiss Pavilion at Expo 2020 Dubai is externally
  documented. Client scale publicly verifiable — Lugano 1906, 16 countries, six
  manufacturing sites, supplying 100+.
- **Must show:** privacy as a physical product, not a promise. One system across
  boutique, campaign and atelier.
- **Strengthen with:** one checkable fact — pavilion dates, coverage, retail outcome.

### Forrey & Galland — Practices 02 + 05
- **Strongest of the three**, because it demonstrates breadth *under discipline* — the
  deck's central claim. Dubai maison since 1912; seasonal collections, co-branded
  partnership and two live D2C webshops on one calendar.
- **Must show:** the calendar as organising device; both webshops named so they can be
  visited; Huda Beauty as earned reach planned, not bought.
- **Must avoid:** splitting into three stories. Its value is that they ran as one.
- **Strengthen with:** commercial permission — most likely of the three to support a
  defendable number, and it is EOV's own work.

## 6. Three things to settle before build

1. **Does eov.ae currently meet the Practice 03 standard?** Could not be inspected —
   the domain has been blocked by network policy throughout this project. Slide 10
   invites scrutiny of EOV's own platform: powerful if exemplary, damaging if not.
2. **Can Ferronato or Forrey & Galland carry one defendable commercial number?** Three
   cases with no figures reads as craft rather than results. One verifiable number
   would settle it — and these are EOV's own work, so the number is EOV's to state.
3. **Is the government roster nameable?** Act 4 rests one institutional credential on
   OPEC. If entities from the 32+ projects can be named, Act 4 gains a second
   institutional case and the positioning question reopens in EOV's favour.
