## 1 · The government picture was under-claimed, not over-claimed

`24` recorded "no nameable government case" and recommended the deck stop
implying a roster. **That was wrong in one direction.** EOV publishes a
20-mark logo wall on its own homepage today.

**Sixteen public-sector marks** (read from image filenames — attribution
inferred, not confirmed): OPEC · SPSA · Mubadala · MOPA · ADAEGP · ADNOC ·
RTA · Department of Economic Development · POD/DCD · Pension Fund · FCA ·
Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority · ESMA · Ministry of Energy ·
DEWA · Ministry of Economy.

**Private, three:** HMS · DAMAC · MBR.
**Partners (About page):** Marcollin · an SAA mark · Palo Alto Tribunal.
**Named in body copy:** OPEC, and UAE Night Vienna, described as organised in
partnership with H.E. Suhail Al Mazrouei, Minister of Energy.

**The caveat is real and load-bearing.** A logo on a 2022 website is not
consent to name a client in a 2026 credentials deck, and several of these are
federal entities with strict naming rules. But it is useful leverage in the
consent conversation, and it means the ask to Mohamed is now sharper:

> **Which of these were contracted to EOV directly, which were subcontracted,
> and which are on the wall because someone liked the logo?**

The three answers get very different treatment in the deck.

| **Existing deck** | Five practices |
| **`25` draft** | Six disciplines |

A prospect who checks the site before a meeting sees a different company from

The site's own deliverable lists are detailed and usable as raw material —
e.g. Digital Presence covers branding and guidelines, campaign design, media
buying, reporting, copywriting, visual content, videography and animation,
virtual reality, content planning and management.

**Note:** the site sells **videography, animation and virtual reality** and
carries **no video on any of its 12 pages**. Do not claim video capability in
the deck without assets to show.

## 4 · Claims on the site — the 400+ trap

Three unsourced claims, all on the About page:

- **32+** — "over 32 Key Government and Private sector project in innovation
  technology and Digital Identity Transformation"
- **400+** — presented as scale, qualified in the copy as *"expanding teams
  and partnerships"*
- **Best Marketing & Promotion Business Award** — no issuer, no year

**400+ must not be imported into the deck.** It reads as headcount at a
glance and is qualified as partnerships. That is precisely the question buyers
ask first, and the follow-up kills it. If the truth is a small core team plus a
partner bench, saying that plainly is stronger.

**The award is unresolved.** The site shows it exactly as the deck does — no
issuer, no year. Binary: produce the certificate or remove it.

## 5 · Live liabilities — the most urgent item in the programme

Sending a deck ahead of a meeting guarantees the recipient searches for EOV.
Today they find:

1. **The Careers page is an unedited Wix template.** "We're based in San
   Francisco's innovation hub." Four San Francisco job posts — Electrical
   Engineer, Data Scientist, AI Researcher, Deep Learning Engineer — with body
   copy still reading *"I'm a paragraph. Click here to add your own text and
   edit me."* Live for at least two years.
2. **Typos in headline copy.** "ONE HAND DOSE NOT CLAP" as a section header ·
   "YORU DIGITAL PLANS" · "TECHNOLOHGY" · "an eye of an Expect" ·
   "Business Inquires" · "your product and servicer".
3. **Copyright reads 2022**, on a site claiming to help clients thrive
   digitally. Sitemap last modified September 2024.

**Unpublishing the Careers page and the four job posts is a five-minute job in
the Wix editor.** It is worth more than any slide in this programme: it costs
a prospect nothing to find and it is unanswerable in a meeting.

## 6 · Origin line needs clarifying

Verbatim from About, capitals as published:

> "ESTABLISHED IN 2006, WHO SNAPPED UP A LONG-STANDING DIGITAL MEDIA AND
> TECHNOLOGY AGENCY SPECIALIZE IN UNIQUE BRAND BUILDING…"

Grammatically broken and ambiguous. Does EOV date from 2006, or did it acquire
an agency that did? "Since 2006" appears on slides 01 and 02 of `25`, so it
needs one clear answer.

Also confirms the descriptor conflict from `17`: the site says **"digital
media and technology agency"**, the deck says integrated marketing agency.

## 7 · Images — gap unchanged, better route identified

76 unique files across 12 pages: About 28 · Digital Presence 20 · Digital
Solutions 11 · Home 8 · Innovation 6 · Careers 2 · Contact 1.

Mostly stock photography and interface decoration. Roughly **25–30 files that
look like real EOV work**, sitting in two unlabelled galleries with no
captions, no client attribution and no dates. **No video anywhere.** *(WRONG — see `27`.)*

That is a mood board, not a credentials deck. **Item 1 stays blocking.**

Two faster routes than the public site:
1. **EOV's Wix Media Manager** — original uploads at full resolution, with
   dates, usually holding more files than were ever placed on a page. One
   login, and authoritative.
2. **The delivery archive** — event photography, campaign shots and platform
   screens almost never live on a marketing site.

A download script and URL list (Wix resizing parameters stripped, so originals
rather than displayed sizes) came with the extract. **It cannot run from this
environment** — static.wixstatic.com returns 403 at the proxy, same as eov.ae.
It must be run locally.
