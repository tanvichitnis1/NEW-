## 1. The structural decision

Buyers in this category are **information-saturated and confidence-starved**. That makes
the conventional credentials running order actively wrong — it opens with the agency and
asks the reader to care before giving them a reason to.

> **The deck earns attention by demonstrating fluency in the buyer's world before it
> says anything about EOV.**

| Conventional order | This deck |
|---|---|
| Who we are | The shift in your world |
| What we do — the service list | The gap it creates |
| Our clients | **EOV — and why it fits the gap** |
| Case studies | How we work — the method |
| Contact | Proof |
| | Where we would start |

Second structural consequence: **the five practices never appear as a list.** They appear
inside the delivery model as the work of each stage. That single move converts a menu
into a method — and the menu is the thing the research says cannot carry an argument.

## 4. Headline read-through

The structural test: read only the headlines and see whether the argument holds. It does —
which is why headlines get written first and slide content written to them, never the reverse.

1. We know how the work gets approved — not just how it gets made.
2. Government communication in the UAE is now a regulated practice.
3. Frameworks. Coordination. Approval platforms. Procurement scoring.
4. Most agencies learn this at your expense.
5. The ones who advise don't build. The ones who build don't know institutions.
6. We do both halves of the job.
7. Emirati-owned. Inside UAE institutions since 2006.
8. Decisions before spend.
9. Foundation — what are we actually solving?
10. Launch — built, approved, live.
11. Always-On — run, measured, renewed.
12. Twenty years. [n] institutions.
13. Case one. Case two. Case three.
14. The people who present are the people who deliver.

## 5. Content hierarchy rules

- **One idea per slide, stated in the headline.** Two headlines means two slides.
- **Three levels, never four.** Headline carries the claim, one line qualifies, evidence beneath.
- **Every number defendable under one follow-up question**, or cut. Asymmetric downside.
- **No slide contains a list of services.** Capability appears only as the work of a stage.
- **Cases follow one fixed shape** so the reader learns the pattern once.
- **Named people, named responsibilities.**
- **Nothing appears twice.** Repetition reads as padding.

## 6. Variants

- **Sent ahead (17 slides).** Full structure. Must pre-qualify EOV with nobody in the room.
- **In the room (7–8 slides).** 01, 05, 06, 08, one case, 16, 17. First meetings are
  discovery conversations, not presentations — the short cut exists to leave room for
  the conversation.

**Segment variants reorder the opening only.** Private-sector audiences: Movement 1
reframes from government regulation to institutional standards; Movement 4 moves ahead of
Movement 3. Movements 5 and 6 never change.

## 7. Blocked — 9 of 17 slides

Movements 1, 2, 4 and 6 are largely writable now. **Movement 5 is entirely blocked, and
it is the movement that does the convincing.**

- **02–03** need the primary Dubai and UAE GMO communication guides
- **12–15** need the citable roster — the single largest blocker in the project
- **07** needs ICV certification status
- **16** needs bios for people actually on accounts

**If the roster proves largely unnameable, Movement 5 must be redesigned rather than
written** — anonymised sector cases, method-led proof, third-party validation. That is a
structural decision, which is why it needs answering before drafting rather than during.
