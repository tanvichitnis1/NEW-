# 29 · What an Agency Profile Needs — and where ours falls short

**Artifact:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4e71770d-2772-4464-ac1d-d5fdb2f470bf

Reviewed against pitch-consultancy guidance and buyer-side RFI practice.
Audits the deck copy in `25` against the standard inventory.

---

## 1 · The job

> To move the audience from unknowing to knowing, in a manner that legitimises
> you and reduces the risk of entering a relationship with you.

**Risk reduction, not persuasion.** The buyer is not deciding whether
marketing is good; they are deciding whether hiring you is survivable.
Everything lowering perceived risk earns its slide; everything merely
asserting quality does not.

Recurring criticism across sources: agencies produce *generic company profiles
that list services, show a few credentials, and end with a weak "contact us"* —
no clear value, no differentiation, nothing addressed to the buyer's situation.

## 2 · Twelve recurring components, EOV's status

| # | Component | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | **Have** | Name, what the company is, contact |
| 2 | **Point of view** | **Wrong order** | Guidance is explicit: winning decks open on a POV, **not founding-year facts**; skip origin story and values |
| 3 | Services and capabilities | **Have** | In-house vs partnered stated plainly |
| 4 | Approach / methodology | **Have** | Should carry a simple visual diagram, not prose |
| 5 | Case studies, 3–5 | **Weak** | Standard is challenge → approach → work → **measurable result**. **Ferronato carries no result and leads** |
| 6 | Clients | **Have** | Strong once consent lands — 16 public-sector marks plus DAMAC, HMS |
| 7 | **Testimonials** | **MISSING** | Cheapest credibility instrument there is; a competitor cannot fabricate it. Never asked for |
| 8 | Team | **Have** | Headshots, two-line specialist bios |
| 9 | **Awards / press / partnerships** | **MISSING** | EOV has an unused partner row: Marcollin, an SAA mark, Palo Alto Tribunal |
| 10 | Engagement models | **Thin** | "How to start" exists; no commercial shape at all |
| 11 | **Conflicts of interest** | **MISSING** | Buyer-side RFI practice looks for it, with contactable references. Trust signal *because* volunteering it is unusual |
| 12 | Next step | **Have** | Named person with authority to scope and price |

**7 have · 2 weak · 3 missing.**

Also noted: **10–15 slides is the consensus sweet spot** — `25`'s 14–16 sits
correctly.

## 3 · Six changes

1. **Move the POV in front of the company facts.** `25` opens cover → who we
   are (Emirati-founded, since 2006) → the problem. Flip 02 and 03 so it opens
   on *"most companies buy marketing in pieces, and nobody is answerable for
   the whole."* Costs nothing; changes the first thing the reader meets from a
   fact about EOV to a fact about themselves.
2. **Get a result onto Ferronato, or move it out of first position.** F&G has
   three figures, KGS two, **Ferronato none** — and it leads. Either find one
   (retail doors, market entries, sell-through, coverage) or promote F&G to
   first and let Ferronato follow as the market-entry story.
3. **Ask for three testimonials this week.** One government, one commercial,
   one long-standing. Two sentences each. Ask the clients whose work is already
   documented. A different and easier ask than naming consent — same email.
4. **Use the partner row.** Marcollin, SAA, Palo Alto Tribunal are on EOV's own
   About page and have never appeared in a deck. The standing objection to a
   mid-size agency is capacity; named partners answer it.
5. **Add one slide on commercial structure.** Retainer, project or staged; what
   a first engagement looks like in shape, not price. An agency that cannot
   answer reads as inexperienced or evasive.
6. **One line on conflicts, for the tender version.** How competing accounts in
   a category are handled.

## 4 · The finding that cuts against all of it

The same source notes that in a credentials meeting, agencies **should resist
talking about themselves and ask questions instead** — the ability to listen
is itself being assessed.

Not an argument against a strong document; an argument about what it is *for*.
**Sent ahead, it does the talking so the meeting does not have to.** If EOV
arrives and presents these fifteen slides aloud, the document has failed at its
actual job.

This supports the sequence already in place (`25`): send ahead, spend the
meeting on their problem.
